# Spider Robot Walking Principle: Theoretical Reference & Engineering Manual

**Target Hardware:** ESP32 DevKit + PCA9685 (I2C 0x40, SDA=21, SCL=22) + 12 Servos (3-DOF per leg)  
**Confirmed Link Lengths:** Coxa $L_c = 42.2\text{ mm}$, Femur $L_f = 65.4\text{ mm}$, Tibia $L_t = 97.5\text{ mm}$  

---

## 1. Robot Coordinate Frames & Convention

### 1.1 Body Reference Frame $\{B\}$
The primary inertial reference frame of the quadruped chassis is defined as:
- **Origin $(0, 0, 0)$**: Geometric center of the chassis between the four hip rotation axes.
- **$+X_B$**: Longitudinal forward direction.
- **$-X_B$**: Longitudinal backward direction.
- **$+Y_B$**: Lateral left direction.
- **$-Y_B$**: Lateral right direction.
- **$+Z_B$**: Vertical upward direction (normal to ground plane).
- **$Z = 0$**: Ground contact plane.

### 1.2 Local Leg Reference Frame $\{L_i\}$
Each leg $i \in \{0, 1, 2, 3\}$ has a local coordinate frame centered at its coxa yaw axis:
- **Leg 0 (Front Left, FL)**: Hip at $(+L_{\text{body}}/2, +W_{\text{body}}/2, 0)$
- **Leg 1 (Front Right, FR)**: Hip at $(+L_{\text{body}}/2, -W_{\text{body}}/2, 0)$
- **Leg 2 (Back Left, BL)**: Hip at $(-L_{\text{body}}/2, +W_{\text{body}}/2, 0)$
- **Leg 3 (Back Right, BR)**: Hip at $(-L_{\text{body}}/2, -W_{\text{body}}/2, 0)$

Within the local leg frame:
- **$+x$**: Forward displacement relative to hip pivot (parallel to $+X_B$).
- **$+y$**: Lateral outward displacement from body center ($+y > 0$ always points outward toward the side).
- **$+z$**: Vertical elevation relative to hip pivot ($z < 0$ when foot contacts ground below chassis).

### 1.3 Four Distinct Joint Angles
To avoid conflating coordinate systems during calibration, four quantities are strictly distinguished:
1. **Mathematical Kinematic Angle**: Pure theoretical Cartesian joint angle computed from trigonometry.
2. **Physical Link Angle**: Real physical orientation of the 3D-printed link relative to the chassis frame.
3. **Servo Command Angle**: Scaled angular target in degrees passed into calibration software ($0^\circ - 180^\circ$).
4. **Calibrated Reference Position**: The physical potentiometer center and mounting offset recorded for that individual servo.

## 2. Walking Principle & Gait Timing

Locomotion is generated by cyclically coordinating the stance and swing phases across all four legs.

### 2.1 Stance Phase
- The foot remains planted on the ground plane ($z = z_0$).
- The foot translates backward relative to the chassis ($+L/2 \to -L/2$).
- Under no-slip contact friction, this backward foot motion exerts a forward reaction force on the chassis, propelling the robot forward.

### 2.2 Swing Phase
- The foot lifts off the ground, clears obstacles along an arch of height $H$, travels forward ($-L/2 \to +L/2$), and touches down smoothly to begin the next stance phase.

### 2.3 Normalized Gait Phase Generator
Let:
- $T$: Gait cycle period in seconds (default $2.0\text{ s}$).
- $t$: Continuous elapsed time in seconds.
- $\phi_i$: Leg phase offset in radians.
- $\beta$: Stance duty factor (default $0.70$).

The normalized master phase $u_i \in [0.0, 1.0)$ for leg $i$ is:
$$u_i = \text{fractional\_part}\left(\frac{t}{T} + \frac{\phi_i}{2\pi}\right)$$

#### Diagonal Trot Gait ($\beta = 0.70$):
- **Group A (FL and BR)**: $\phi_0 = 0\text{ rad}, \phi_3 = 0\text{ rad} \implies \frac{\phi}{2\pi} = 0.0$
- **Group B (FR and BL)**: $\phi_1 = \pi\text{ rad}, \phi_2 = \pi\text{ rad} \implies \frac{\phi}{2\pi} = 0.5$
- **Quadruple Stance Overlap**: Because $\beta = 0.70 > 0.50$, both Group A and Group B overlap in stance for $20\%$ of each half-cycle ($[0.30, 0.50]$ and $[0.80, 1.00]$). All 4 feet are firmly planted on the floor during these intervals, stabilizing pitch and roll before alternating diagonal pairs lift.

#### 4-Beat Crawl Gait ($\beta = 0.75$):
- Sequence: Back Left (2) $\to$ Front Left (0) $\to$ Back Right (3) $\to$ Front Right (1).
- Offsets: $\phi_2 = 0.00, \phi_0 = 0.25, \phi_3 = 0.50, \phi_1 = 0.75$.
- Stance duration: $75\%$; Swing duration: $25\%$.
- **Static Stability Guarantee**: Exactly one leg swings at any given instant. Three legs are always planted on the ground, forming a static support tripod containing the Center of Mass.

## 3. Gait Trajectory Equations

### 3.1 Stance Trajectory ($0 \le u < \beta$)
Define normalized stance progress $s = u / \beta \in [0.0, 1.0]$:
$$x(s) = +\frac{L}{2} - L \cdot s$$
$$z(s) = z_0 \quad (\text{ground contact})$$
- At $s = 0.0$ ($u = 0.0$): $x = +L/2$ (forward touchdown placement).
- At $s = 0.5$: $x = 0$ (directly in line with hip).
- At $s = 1.0$ ($u = \beta$): $x = -L/2$ (rear stance extension).

### 3.2 Swing Trajectory ($\beta \le u < 1.0$)
Define normalized swing progress $q = \frac{u - \beta}{1 - \beta} \in [0.0, 1.0]$:
$$x(q) = -\frac{L}{2} + L \cdot (3q^2 - 2q^3)$$
$$z(q) = z_0 + H \cdot \sin(\pi q)$$

#### Boundary & Velocity Continuity Analysis:
- At liftoff ($q = 0.0$):
  $$x(0) = -\frac{L}{2}, \quad z(0) = z_0, \quad \left.\frac{dx}{dq}\right|_{q=0} = L(6q - 6q^2)_{q=0} = 0$$
- At apex ($q = 0.5$):
  $$x(0.5) = 0, \quad z(0.5) = z_0 + H$$
- At touchdown ($q = 1.0$):
  $$x(1) = +\frac{L}{2}, \quad z(1) = z_0, \quad \left.\frac{dx}{dq}\right|_{q=1} = L(6q - 6q^2)_{q=1} = 0$$

*Result:* The cubic polynomial smoothstep $S(q) = 3q^2 - 2q^3$ guarantees zero horizontal velocity at both liftoff and touchdown, eliminating the foot-drag and gear-stripping shock loads common in naive controllers.

## 4. Analytical 3-DOF Inverse Kinematics

### 4.1 Planar Transformation & Geometric Reach Metric $D$
Given foot target $(x, y, z)$ in the local leg frame:
1. **Coxa Yaw Angle**:
   $$\rho = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}, \quad \theta_{\text{coxa}} = \arctan2(x, y)$$
2. **Radial Distance in Leg Pitch Plane**:
   The coxa length $L_c = 42.2\text{ mm}$ extends horizontally and is subtracted first:
   $$r = \rho - L_c = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2} - 42.2\text{ mm}$$
   *(If $r < 1.0\text{ mm}$, clamp to $r = 1.0\text{ mm}$).*
3. **Planar Distance Squared ($d^2$)**:
   $$d^2 = r^2 + z^2 \implies d = \sqrt{r^2 + z^2}$$
4. **Geometric Reach Check Metric ($D$)**:
   $$D = \frac{r^2 + z^2 - L_f^2 - L_t^2}{2 L_f L_t} = \frac{d^2 - 65.4^2 - 97.5^2}{2 \times 65.4 \times 97.5} = \frac{d^2 - 13783.41}{12753.0}$$

### 4.2 Reachability Criteria & Theoretical Distance Bounds
- Target is reachable if and only if **$-1.0 \le D \le 1.0$**.
- **Theoretical Minimum Reach:** $|L_t - L_f| = |97.5 - 65.4| = 32.1\text{ mm}$ ($D = -1.0$).
- **Theoretical Maximum Reach:** $L_f + L_t = 65.4 + 97.5 = 162.9\text{ mm}$ ($D = +1.0$).
- Out-of-reach targets are safely clamped to $D_{\text{clamped}} = \text{constrain}(D, -1.0, 1.0)$ to prevent arithmetic faults in trigonometric functions.

### 4.3 Knee and Femur Angles
$$\theta_{\text{knee}} = \arctan2\left(\sqrt{1 - D_{\text{clamped}}^2}, D_{\text{clamped}}\right)$$
$$\beta_{\text{femur}} = \arctan2\left(L_t \sin(\theta_{\text{knee}}), L_f + L_t \cos(\theta_{\text{knee}})\right)$$
$$\theta_{\text{femur}} = \arctan2(z, r) + \beta_{\text{femur}}$$

*Branch Explanation:* Adding $\beta_{\text{femur}}$ selects the elevated-knee / outward-elbow spider configuration matching the physical 3D-printed chassis. Subtracting $\beta_{\text{femur}}$ yields the inverted/tucked-under configuration.

## 5. Forward Kinematics Verification Results

Forward Kinematics reconstructs Cartesian coordinates from joint angles:
$$\theta_{\text{tibia\_world}} = \theta_{\text{femur}} - \theta_{\text{knee}}$$
$$r = L_f \cos(\theta_{\text{femur}}) + L_t \cos(\theta_{\text{tibia\_world}}), \quad z = L_f \sin(\theta_{\text{femur}}) + L_t \sin(\theta_{\text{tibia\_world}})$$
$$\rho = L_c + r, \quad x = \rho \sin(\theta_{\text{coxa}}), \quad y = \rho \cos(\theta_{\text{coxa}})$$

Reconstruction error $\Delta E = \sqrt{(x - x_{\text{recon}})^2 + (y - y_{\text{recon}})^2 + (z - z_{\text{recon}})^2}$:
- **Nominal Stance $(0, 110, -90)\text{ mm}$**: $\Delta E < 0.0001\text{ mm}$
- **Forward Reach $(25, 110, -90)\text{ mm}$**: $\Delta E < 0.0001\text{ mm}$
- **Backward Stride $(-25, 110, -90)\text{ mm}$**: $\Delta E < 0.0001\text{ mm}$
- **Swing Apex $(0, 110, -75)\text{ mm}$**: $\Delta E < 0.0001\text{ mm}$
- **Lowered Sit $(0, 125, -40)\text{ mm}$**: $\Delta E < 0.0001\text{ mm}$

## 6. Stability, Balance & Support Polygons

### 6.1 Support Polygon
The support polygon is the convex hull formed by all foot contact points planted on the ground plane:
$$\mathcal{P}_{\text{support}} = \text{ConvexHull}\left(\{ (x_i, y_i) \mid \text{leg } i \text{ in stance} \}\right)$$

### 6.2 Quasi-Static Stability Criterion
A quadruped is stable if and only if the vertical projection of its Center of Mass (CoM) lies strictly within the interior of $\mathcal{P}_{\text{support}}$:
$$\text{CoM}_{xy} \in \text{Interior}(\mathcal{P}_{\text{support}})$$

- In **4-Beat Crawl**, 3 legs are always in stance, creating a large support triangle. Quasi-static stability is maintained throughout the entire gait.
- In **Diagonal Trot**, during the 2-leg swing phase, the support polygon collapses to a diagonal line. Balance is maintained dynamically by keeping cycle periods short ($2.0\text{ s}$ cadence), step heights conservative ($15\text{ mm}$), and utilizing the $20\%$ 4-leg support overlap.

## 7. Mechanical Load & Joint Torque Estimation

### 7.1 Static Load Calculation
For a stationary quadruped of total mass $m$ under gravitational acceleration $g \approx 9.81\text{ m/s}^2$:
$$F_{\text{total}} = m \cdot g$$
Assuming symmetric distribution across 4 legs in nominal stance:
$$F_{\text{leg}} \approx \frac{m \cdot g}{4}$$
During a 2-leg diagonal trot support phase:
$$F_{\text{leg, trot}} \approx \frac{m \cdot g}{2}$$

The static torque $\tau$ exerted on the femur and knee joints depends on the perpendicular lever arm $r_{\perp}$:
$$\tau_{\text{joint}} = F_{\text{leg}} \cdot r_{\perp}$$

*Illustrative Example:* For a $1.2\text{ kg}$ robot ($F_{\text{total}} \approx 11.8\text{ N}$), each diagonal stance leg supports $F_{\text{leg}} \approx 5.9\text{ N}$. At nominal horizontal extension $r \approx 68\text{ mm} = 0.068\text{ m}$:
$$\tau_{\text{femur}} \approx 5.9\text{ N} \times 0.068\text{ m} \approx 0.40\text{ N}\cdot\text{m} \approx 4.1\text{ kg}\cdot\text{cm}$$
Standard MG90S metal-gear micro servos are rated for $1.8 - 2.2\text{ kg}\cdot\text{cm}$ stall torque at 4.8V–6.0V. Operating at $4.1\text{ kg}\cdot\text{cm}$ continuous load would stall the servos!  
**Crucial Engineering Rule:** To keep joint torque within safe servo limits:
1. Keep chassis mass as low as possible ($< 700\text{ g}$).
2. Tuck legs closer to the body (reduce stance radius $y_0$ to shorten lever arm $r_{\perp}$).
3. Dynamic loads (accelerating body mass and landing foot impacts) are $1.5\times - 2.5\times$ greater than static estimates.

## 8. Electrical Power, Sizing & Grounding

1. **Servo Supply Voltage**: Regulated $5.0\text{V} - 6.0\text{V}$ DC connected directly to PCA9685 `V+` screw terminal.
2. **Current Sizing**:
   $$I_{\text{peak, total}} \approx \sum_{i=1}^{12} I_{\text{stall}, i} \approx 12 \times 0.85\text{A} \approx 10.2\text{A}$$
   During walking, 6 to 8 servos draw current simultaneously ($3.0\text{A} - 5.0\text{A}$ sustained). A standard 2A LM2596 buck converter will overheat and collapse. Use a buck converter rated for at least **5A–8A continuous**.
3. **Common Ground**: ESP32 GND, PCA9685 GND, and servo power supply negative must be bonded to a common ground reference.
4. **Buffer Capacitor**: Install a $1000\,\mu\text{F} - 2200\,\mu\text{F}$ low-ESR electrolytic capacitor across the PCA9685 `V+` and `GND` terminals.

## 9. PCA9685 PWM Conversion

At $50\text{ Hz}$ PWM frequency:
- Period: $T_{\text{PWM}} = \frac{1}{50\text{ Hz}} = 0.020\text{ s} = 20{,}000\,\mu\text{s}$.
- 12-bit Counter: $4096$ counts per period.
- Pulse width to ticks conversion:
  $$\text{ticks} = \text{round}\left(\frac{\text{pulse\_width\_us} \times 50 \times 4096}{1{,}000{,}000}\right) = \text{round}\left(\frac{\text{pulse\_width\_us} \times 4096}{20{,}000}\right)$$
  - For $500\,\mu\text{s}$: $\approx 102\text{ counts}$
  - For $1500\,\mu\text{s}$: $\approx 307\text{ counts}$
  - For $2500\,\mu\text{s}$: $\approx 512\text{ counts}$
